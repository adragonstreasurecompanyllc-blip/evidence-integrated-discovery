# Evidence-Integrated Scientific Discovery

**Researcher:** JoAnne Noto  
**ORCID:** https://orcid.org/0009-0008-9466-0961  
**Research Status:** Proposed — not yet experimentally validated  
**Research Area:** Computational Biology, AI-Assisted Scientific Discovery, Metabolic Disease

## Research Overview

This project proposes a modular, evidence-integrated artificial intelligence framework for investigating complex biological questions.

The central research hypothesis is that combining multiple scientific evidence types, adversarial reasoning, transparent evidence provenance, and adjustable AI autonomy can improve measurable scientific discovery performance compared with simpler AI research workflows.

## Initial Scientific Focus

The initial 8–12-week proof of concept will investigate metabolic dysfunction, with particular attention to insulin resistance, diabetes, and kidney stone formation.

The project will examine established biological relationships, conflicting findings, and unresolved mechanistic questions using publicly available scientific resources.

## Research Architecture

The proposed Python-based framework will integrate:

1. **Evidence Integration:** Connect published literature, biological datasets, clinical research, and molecular information.
2. **Hypothesis Generation:** Identify potential biological relationships and formulate testable scientific explanations.
3. **Adversarial Scientific Review:** Evaluate competing hypotheses, contradictory evidence, and methodological limitations.
4. **Evidence Provenance:** Trace scientific claims to their original sources and computational analyses.
5. **Adjustable Autonomy:** Support researcher-directed, collaborative, and more autonomous computational workflows with human oversight.

## Experimental Methodology

The initial investigation will use four complementary validation components:

- Independent evaluation using held-out public biological datasets.
- Historical rediscovery benchmarks with explicit safeguards against information leakage.
- Reproducible computational verification using established Python scientific libraries.
- Integrated assessment of discovery accuracy, scientific reliability, computational performance, and scientific usefulness.

The integrated framework will be compared against simpler AI research workflows.

## Proposed Deliverables

Within an 8–12-week research collaboration, the project aims to produce:

- A modular Python scientific discovery prototype.
- A reproducible historical discovery benchmark.
- A comparative scientific and computational evaluation.
- A preliminary investigation of selected metabolic disease mechanisms.
- A research manuscript documenting findings, limitations, and future directions.

## Long-Term Vision

The long-term objective is a disease-agnostic computational discovery framework that can investigate biological questions across multiple disease families.

## Research Integrity and Data Use

The project will use publicly available scientific information and appropriately licensed research resources. It will not include private patient data, confidential institutional records, or proprietary healthcare software.

Computationally generated hypotheses will not be represented as established biological discoveries without appropriate validation.

## Institutional and Collaboration Status

This project was independently conceived and is currently in the proposal stage. Formal institutional research sponsorship, external collaboration, and research funding have not been confirmed.
